Artemis II

NASA's Artemis II was the first crewed flight test of the Space Launch System rocket and Orion spacecraft.

Mission Overview

The Artemis II mission launched on April 1, 2026, carrying four astronauts on a looping journey around the Moon. The crew included NASA astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, and Christina Koch, alongside Canadian Space Agency astronaut Jeremy Hansen. This flight marked the first time humans traveled toward the Moon since the end of the Apollo program in 1972.

Journey and Record

The spacecraft traveled past the Moon on a free-return trajectory, reaching a distance of over 252,000 miles from Earth. This set a new record for the furthest distance any human has ever traveled in space. The mission lasted nearly 10 days, concluding with a safe splashdown in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of San Diego on April 10, 2026.
